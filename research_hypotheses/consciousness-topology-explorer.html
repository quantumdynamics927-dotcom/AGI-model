Scientific Topology Explorer

Consciousness topology as a measurable graph hypothesis.

This standalone explorer compares three structural models — linear stream, 4D hypercube, and fractal branching — using computed graph-theoretic metrics rather than fixed narrative scores. It is designed as a structural thought experiment: useful for measuring connectivity, routing efficiency, and resilience, but not as direct evidence that consciousness literally has any one topology.

Method boundary

These metrics evaluate graph structure only. A graph can be efficient without corresponding to phenomenology, neural anatomy, or causal consciousness. Use this tool to compare models, not to claim proof.

Derived comparison logic

Low average path length implies rapid multi-state reachability High efficiency implies strong nonlocal routing potential High clustering implies local closure and motif reuse Low lesion robustness signals central bottlenecks

A more scientific consciousness claim would require empirical state-transition data, explicit mechanisms, and failure conditions. This explorer improves the topology argument by turning it into a measurable graph comparison instead of a purely symbolic diagram.
